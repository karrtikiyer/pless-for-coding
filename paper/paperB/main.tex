\title{When a Hyperparameter-Free Decoder Fails:\\
Diagnosing and Repairing p-less Sampling Loops\\ in Reasoning-Model Code Generation}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Hyperparameter-free decoders such as p-less sampling promise to remove temperature and top-$p$ tuning
by deriving a truncation threshold---the collision probability $\tsum_i p_i^2$---directly from the
token distribution. We show this promise fails silently on reasoning models generating code. On APPS
with two 8B reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill, Qwen3), default p-less sends 15--42\% of
chain-of-thought traces into non-terminating loops---the worst pass@1 of any decoder we test---and
does so at temperature $1.0$, where temperature sampling does not loop. We trace this to a decoder-side
mechanism: on the peaked distributions of long reasoning, the collision threshold rises to meet the top
token, collapsing the survivor set to a single token and re-deriving the same step. A taxonomy shows
roughly half the loops are paraphrastic---semantically redundant but lexically distinct---and are
missed by the verbatim and hidden-state detectors validated only on math. Raising the power-sum
exponent ($\tau_\alpha=\tsum_i p_i^\alpha$; a frequency-moment threshold, \emph{not} a R\'enyi-entropy
generalization) removes the loops, recovers accuracy, and halves wasted tokens, matching or beating
each model's recommended settings out of the box---though not a hand-tuned temperature. p-less-$\alpha$
is a diagnostic and repair lever.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Hyperparameter-free decoders are appealing because they remove the temperature/top-$p$/top-$k$ tuning
that otherwise depends on the model, task, and desired diversity. \citet{tan2025pless} propose
\textbf{p-less} and \textbf{p-less-norm}, truncation samplers whose admission threshold is the
collision probability $\tsum_i p_i^2$ of the distribution, with no tunable knobs, and evaluate them on
math, reasoning, and creative writing. We ask what happens on reasoning models generating
code---a setting the origin paper does not study, and one where the long, low-entropy thinking phase
stresses any decoder.

We find that p-less \emph{fails silently} there. On APPS with two 8B reasoning models, default p-less
sends 15--42\% of chain-of-thought traces into non-terminating loops that run to the token cap,
producing the worst pass@1 of any decoder we test---while emitting no error and, at first glance, no
obvious pathology. Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{The silent failure} (\S\ref{sec:failure}): default p-less loops on reasoning-model
    code CoT, at temperature $1.0$, where temperature sampling does not.
  \item \textbf{A decoder-side mechanism} (\S\ref{sec:mech}): on peaked distributions the collision
    threshold meets the top probability, collapsing the survivor set to the single most-probable
    token exactly where the model is most confident.
  \item \textbf{A code loop taxonomy} (\S\ref{sec:tax}): $\sim$half of the loops are paraphrastic
    (semantic drift), missed by verbatim-periodicity and hidden-state-precursor detectors; a published
    precursor does not transfer to code.
  \item \textbf{The $\alpha$-power-sum lever} (\S\ref{sec:alpha}): raising the exponent
    $\tau_\alpha=\tsum_i p_i^\alpha$ removes loops, recovers accuracy, roughly halves thinking tokens,
    and on the heavy looper is the most token-efficient decoder.
  \item \textbf{The $\alpha$ lever in context} (\S\ref{sec:pos}): $\alpha$ beats each model's recommended
    settings with zero tuning but does not beat a hand-swept temperature; and $\tau_\alpha$ is a
    power-sum threshold, not a R\'enyi-entropy generalization.
\end{enumerate}
The value of a hyperparameter-free default is that it works out of the box; our point is that this one
silently does not, on an increasingly common setting, and that a single exponent both explains and
repairs the failure.

\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:bg}
\paragraph{p-less and p-less-norm.} Given a distribution $p$ over a vocabulary of size $v$, p-less
admits tokens with $p_i \ge \tsum_j p_j^2$; p-less-norm relaxes the threshold to
$(v\tsum_j p_j^2 - 1)/(v-1)$. Survivors are renormalized and sampled; the only optional control is a
pre-truncation temperature~\citep{tan2025pless}.

\paragraph{Reasoning-model looping.} \citet{pipis2025loop} show reasoning models loop at low
temperature, attributing it to risk aversion---a \emph{model-side} propensity we cite rather than
claim. \citet{duan2026circular} characterize self-reinforcing loops as semantically redundant yet
lexically distinct and predict them from hidden-state transitions; \citet{xie2025wordsalad} note
reasoning models ``do not exhibit strictly verbatim repetitions'' and catch the resulting text with a
hidden-state probe. \citet{yang2025recover} show models identify but fail to recover from unhelpful
thoughts, with inverse scaling; DEER~\citep{yang2025deer} and s1~\citep{muennighoff2025s1} control
reasoning length reactively or by budget forcing. \textbf{All of this work is on math/QA and is
detection- or length-focused}: none evaluates code, none reports the fraction of loops that are
paraphrastic, and none studies a truncation sampler as a \emph{cause} or \emph{preventive} of
looping---the gaps we fill.

\paragraph{Truncation and degeneration.} \citet{holtzman2020curious} established that
likelihood-maximizing decoding produces repetitive, degenerate text; min-$p$
\citep{nguyen2024minp}, top-$n\sigma$~\citep{tang2024topnsigma}, and $\eta$-sampling
\citep{hewitt2022truncation} are knobbed truncation samplers. Our mechanism (\S\ref{sec:mech}) can be
seen as a truncation-sampler instance of that effect: because $\tsum_i p_i^2 \le \max_i p_i$, on
peaked steps the p-less threshold admits only the most-probable token, so a purely distribution-derived
cutoff reproduces the repetition that maximization-based decoding is known to cause.

\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Models, data, decoders.} We use DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B~\citep{deepseekai2025r1} and
Qwen3-8B~\citep{yang2025qwen3} on 252 APPS ``interview'' problems~\citep{hendrycks2021apps}\footnote{APPS
interview split; problems executed against hidden tests.}, thinking enabled, 10 samples/problem. We
compare p-less ($\alpha{=}2$), p-less-norm, the $\alpha$-family ($\alpha{=}3,4,5$), temperature /
top-$p$ / top-$k$ configurations including each model's officially recommended setting, and an adaptive
detect-and-chop rescue.

\paragraph{Metrics.} pass@1/@10 with the unbiased estimator of \citet{chen2021humaneval};
\emph{non-termination rate} (fraction of samples that never close the thinking phase within the token
budget---our loop proxy); CodeBLEU-based diversity~\citep{ren2020codebleu}; and mean thinking tokens.
Mean tokens count truncated samples at their cut length, so they are biased upward for
high-truncation configs---which is exactly the wasted-budget cost we want to expose.

\paragraph{The $\alpha$ family.} We study $\tau_\alpha(p)=\tsum_i p_i^\alpha$ with $\alpha\ge 2$;
$\alpha{=}2$ is exactly p-less. This is a \emph{power-sum (frequency-moment)} threshold; its relation
to R\'enyi entropy---and its non-equivalence to the origin paper's appendix proposal---is deferred to
\S\ref{sec:pos}.

\section{The silent failure}\label{sec:failure}
Table~\ref{tab:main} reports the full decoder comparison. Default p-less ($\alpha{=}2$) and
p-less-norm are the worst configurations on DeepSeek (pass@1 $0.392$ vs.\ $0.480$ for the best
temperature) and among the worst on Qwen ($0.625$), driven by \textbf{non-termination rates of 41.8\%
(DeepSeek) and 14.5\% (Qwen)}: nearly half of DeepSeek's reasoning traces loop until the token cap.

The failure is easy to misattribute. Reasoning models are known to loop at low
temperature~\citep{pipis2025loop}, so one might dismiss this as the same effect, or attribute the
temperature configs' stability to their top-$p$/top-$k$ truncation. Figure~\ref{fig:failuret1} rules
out both: p-less loops on 15--42\% of traces \emph{at temperature $1.0$}, while every
temperature-family config stays near 0\%---including temperature with no top-$p$/top-$k$
(1.8\% DeepSeek, 0.4\% Qwen). At a matched temperature the contrast is direct: on Qwen
at $T{=}0.6$, p-less loops on 19.0\% of traces versus 0.4\% for plain temperature. The pathology is
therefore \emph{structural to the p-less threshold}---neither a low-temperature effect nor a
consequence of secondary truncation---and it is silent: no crash, no warning, just a slow decline into
repetition that consumes the entire token budget.

\section{The mechanism}\label{sec:mech}
Why does a truncation rule cause looping? Because the p-less threshold $\tsum_i p_i^2$ is bounded above
by the top probability, $\tsum_i p_i^2 \le \max_i p_i$, with equality approached as the distribution
concentrates. On the peaked next-token distributions typical of a confident reasoning step, the
threshold rises to meet the mode, so \emph{only the single most-probable token survives}---on such
steps p-less is forced to emit the argmax. Repeatedly emitting the
argmax re-derives the same continuation, and the model re-enters the same reasoning move---a loop. This
inverts the usual reading of truncation as a cure for repetition~\citep{holtzman2020curious}: pinned to
the collision probability, the threshold \emph{amplifies} repetition where the model is confident.

\paragraph{The loop is a joint effect, not a p-less artifact alone.} Self-reinforcing loops are a
documented \emph{model-side} phenomenon in reasoning models: \citet{pipis2025loop} attribute them to
risk aversion and temporally-correlated errors arising in training (not to the decoder), and
\citet{duan2026circular} study them as an internal, self-reinforcing dynamic. The propensity is
inherited from RL-based reasoning training---e.g.\ DeepSeek-R1's reinforcement learning
\citep{deepseekai2025r1}, distilled into the R1-Distill model we use. p-less does not \emph{create} it:
on the peaked steps these models produce, it admits only the top token and removes the sampling
diversity that lets temperature escape, converting a latent, training-induced tendency into an actual
non-terminating loop. Our contribution is the decoder-side half of this interaction---we take the
loop-propensity as given (its training-side origin we do not investigate) and show that a
collision-probability threshold triggers it on 15--42\% of traces.

\section{A taxonomy of the loops}\label{sec:tax}
What do these loops look like, and can existing detectors catch them? Categorizing every
non-terminating trace (Figure~\ref{fig:tax}), only $\sim$41--50\% are \emph{verbatim} statement loops
(Qwen 40.7\%, DeepSeek 49.8\%)---the periodic, cleanly repeating kind prior work
studies~\citep{duan2026circular,xie2025wordsalad}. A comparable share---\textbf{41.3\% (Qwen) and
46.8\% (DeepSeek)}---are \emph{paraphrastic}: \emph{aperiodic} repetition in which the same idea
recurs under drifting framing, so no clean fixed-period unit emerges (periodicity self-match median
$0.23$--$0.26$; $73$--$74\%$ of these traces below $0.5$; classification method and side-by-side
examples in Appendix~\ref{app:taxonomy}). The remainder are short/degenerate or have no detected loop.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{fig_taxonomy.png}
\caption{Non-terminating traces by type. Roughly half are paraphrastic drift---lexically distinct,
aperiodic---rather than verbatim repetition. Prior loop work, all on math/QA, does not report this
breakdown.}
\label{fig:tax}
\end{figure}

This breakdown matters for detection. Verbatim loops are periodic and are cleanly caught by existing
loop detectors; paraphrastic loops lack that \emph{sustained periodicity}, so the methods built for
verbatim repetition do not fire on them. The hidden-state precursor of \citet{duan2026circular} does
not transfer either: reimplementing it on Qwen3 code traces catches only $\sim$17--20\% of terminal
loops before onset, versus the $0.64$--$0.76$ it reports on synthetic verbatim loops, and its
``semantic-precedes-textual'' precursor is absent before onset. Because paraphrastic loops lack the
clean textual onset the precursor assumes, these rates are directional rather than a strict numerical
comparison; the robust finding is qualitative---roughly half of reasoning-code loops offer no
periodicity or precursor signal to exploit.

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha}-power-sum lever}\label{sec:alpha}
A single exponent repairs the failure. Generalizing the threshold to $\tau_\alpha(p)=\tsum_i p_i^\alpha$
($\alpha{=}2$ is p-less), raising $\alpha$ flattens the survivor set: the threshold shrinks, so more
tail tokens survive even on peaked steps. Across $\alpha=2\to5$ (Table~\ref{tab:main},
Figure~\ref{fig:alpha}) this monotonically (i)~removes looping (non-termination $41.8\%\!\to\!0.3\%$
DeepSeek, $14.5\%\!\to\!0.6\%$ Qwen), (ii)~recovers pass@1 ($0.392\!\to\!0.483$;
$0.625\!\to\!0.696$ at $\alpha{=}4$), (iii)~roughly halves wasted thinking tokens
($17.3\text{k}\!\to\!9.4\text{k}$; $13.5\text{k}\!\to\!11.1\text{k}$), and (iv)~partly restores
diversity (cb\_div $0.489\!\to\!0.553$; $0.453\!\to\!0.474$)---all with no per-task tuning.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_apps_alpha.png}
\caption{Raising $\alpha$ recovers pass@1 (left; dashed lines mark the best temperature config) and
collapses looping (right) from the $\alpha{=}2$ default.}

\paragraph{Token efficiency tracks the loop rate.} Because the savings come from not spending budget on
loops, the token advantage over other decoders scales with how much the model loops
(Table~\ref{tab:main}). On DeepSeek (heavy looper), high-$\alpha$ p-less is the \emph{single most
token-efficient decoder tested}: $\alpha{=}4/5$ use $9.2$k$/9.4$k thinking tokens, fewer than every
temperature/top-$p$/top-$k$ configuration ($9.6$--$10.1$k) at matched-or-better pass@1---and this is
not a truncation artifact ($\alpha{=}5$ truncates $0.3\%$). On Qwen (light looper) it is a tie
($\sim$11.1--11.3k, indistinguishable from temperature; Table~\ref{tab:main}). We therefore do not
claim $\alpha$ is universally more token-efficient; the edge is loop-rate-dependent.

Setting a flatter sampler up front (prevention) also beats detecting and chopping the loop reactively:
prevention ($\alpha{=}5$ throughout) reaches $0.483$ on DeepSeek versus $0.457$ for adaptive
chop-and-continue, and ties on Qwen---the flatter survivor set suppresses re-entry into the loop after
a chop, where $\alpha{=}2$ re-enters.

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha} lever in context}\label{sec:pos}
\paragraph{$\alpha$ vs.\ what a practitioner would actually run.} The right baseline is not a swept
temperature but the setting a user gets out of the box. Against each model's \emph{official recommended}
configuration, the best $\alpha$ wins with zero tuning (Table~\ref{tab:pos}): Qwen $\alpha{=}4$ ($0.696$)
$>$ recommended $T0.6/p0.95/k20$ ($0.680$); DeepSeek $\alpha{=}5$ ($0.483$) $>$ recommended $T0.6/p0.95$
($0.475$). On DeepSeek $\alpha{=}5$ is in fact the single best decoder. p-less-$\alpha$ is outperformed
only by a \emph{higher-temperature} config ($T1.0/p0.95$: Qwen $0.705$, DeepSeek $0.480\approx$ tie)
that departs from the model's own recommendation---i.e.\ found only by the temperature tuning p-less
exists to avoid. Temperature retains a slight diversity edge (cb\_div $0.556$ vs.\ $0.553$ on
DeepSeek). So $\alpha$ is a competitive, calibration-free repair, not a state-of-the-art win.

\paragraph{$\tau_\alpha$ is a power sum, not a R\'enyi generalization.} The origin paper's Appendix~B.5
proposes a different generalization, $G_k=\exp(-H_k)=(\tsum_i p_i^k)^{1/(k-1)}$, purely theoretically:
it analyzes only the asymptotics ($G_k\!\to\!1/n$ as $k\!\to\!0$, $G_k\!\to\!\max_i p_i$ as
$k\!\to\!\infty$) and runs no experiments at $k\neq2$. Our raw power sum $\tau_\alpha=\tsum_i p_i^\alpha$ and their rooted $G_k$ coincide only at
order $2$; away from it they move in \emph{opposite} directions along the order parameter
(Table~\ref{tab:noneq}, Appendix~\ref{app:noneq}): the loosening we obtain by \emph{raising} $\alpha$
above $2$ corresponds, in the rooted form, to \emph{lowering} $k$ below $2$. The correspondence is only
directional---matching the survivor set our sweep reaches at $\alpha{=}5$ would require a R\'enyi order
near $k\!\approx\!-3$ (a \emph{negative} order), and even then the admitted sets differ, since no
order-matching aligns the two families (Appendix~\ref{app:noneq}). The practical reading is that the
power sum is the natural knob for diversity: one raises a single exponent rather than pushing the
R\'enyi entropy to unusual negative orders. We make no optimality claim for $\alpha$ regardless---the
diversity monotonicity it exhibits is shared by top-$p$ and top-$k$, so $\alpha$ is one member of a
broader class.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:disc}
The study covers two 8B reasoning models and one benchmark (APPS); whether the mechanism and the
$\alpha$ lever transfer to other model families and code benchmarks is open. $\alpha$ ties, and does
not beat, the best swept temperature, so this is a diagnosis-and-repair result, not a decoding win.
We characterize the failure as a per-step collapse of the survivor set to the single most-probable
token and do not benchmark greedy decoding itself, so we do not claim p-less reproduces greedy's
sequence-level behavior. We also do
not investigate the model-side origin of the looping propensity---prior work attributes reasoning-model
loops to learning dynamics and inductive biases rather than to the decoder~\citep{pipis2025loop}.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conc}
Hyperparameter-free is not assumption-free. On the peaked distributions of reasoning-model
chain-of-thought, a collision-probability threshold silently collapses its survivor set to the single
most-probable token, driving up to 42\% of code traces into loops---amplifying these reasoning models'
latent, training-induced loop-propensity rather than creating it, by removing the sampling diversity
that lets temperature escape. Raising the power-sum exponent repairs the failure and is competitive out
of the box. The deeper finding is that roughly half of these reasoning-code loops are paraphrastic drift,
invisible to the verbatim and hidden-state detectors validated only on math---a gap current
loop-detection work does not yet address.

\section{Power-sum vs.\ R\'enyi-entropy threshold: non-equivalence}\label{app:noneq}

after (computed).}

Sweeping the order $2\to8$ on $p{=}[0.45,0.25,0.15,0.10,0.05]$, $\tau_\alpha$ admits progressively more
tokens (mode-only up to full support, five distinct survivor sets) while $G_k$ stays locked on the mode
at every order---so no reparameterization $\alpha\leftrightarrow k$ aligns their admitted sets, and the
two families are non-equivalent for order $>2$.

The rooted form loosens only in the \emph{opposite} direction. On $p{=}[0.7,0.2,0.1]$, decreasing the
order gives $G_{1.5}{=}0.50$, $G_{1}{=}0.45$, $G_{0}{=}0.33$ (against $\tau_\alpha = 0.35/0.24/0.17$ at
$\alpha = 3/4/5$); matching our $\alpha{=}5$ threshold $\tau_5 = 0.168$ requires a \emph{negative}
R\'enyi order, $k \approx -3.2$. Raising the power-sum exponent and lowering the R\'enyi order thus
reach qualitatively similar (looser) filters from opposite ends of the order parameter, but never the
same admitted set.

Crucially, that matching order is \emph{not} a constant---it is a function of the whole distribution
and so changes at every decode step. Because $\tau_\alpha = \big(e^{-H_\alpha}\big)^{\alpha-1} =
G_\alpha^{\,\alpha-1}$ (our threshold is the R\'enyi form raised to the fixed power $\alpha-1$),
expressing it as a bare $G_k$ forces the order to absorb a distribution-dependent amount. For a fixed
$\alpha{=}4$, the order matching $\tau_4$ swings with the distribution---for illustration,
$k\approx-1.1$ on $[0.7,0.2,0.1]$ but $k\approx-6.2$ on $[0.6,0.3,0.1]$. There is therefore no fixed
R\'enyi order equivalent to a fixed power-sum $\alpha$: the correspondence is per-distribution, hence
per-step. (Over a full vocabulary, where $\min_i p_i\!\approx\!0$, a matching order almost always
exists---it is simply not constant; the toy claim that none exists is an artifact of small support.)

\section{Loop taxonomy: classification method and examples}\label{app:taxonomy}
\paragraph{Method.} We classify every truncated trace automatically. From the thinking phase we locate
the loop onset with the deployed per-model $n$-gram detector (Qwen3-8B: $n{=}30$, $k{=}6$,
window${=}1200$; DeepSeek-R1-Distill: $n{=}30$, $k{=}6$, window${=}3000$), then search the post-onset
region ($\le1600$ tokens) for a fundamental period $P\le800$ at which the token stream self-matches
$\ge85\%$. A trace is a \emph{verbatim} statement loop if a clean period exists (further split into
``reflective'' if the repeating unit is prose carrying an impasse marker---\emph{wait}, \emph{perhaps},
\emph{stuck}---and ``other'' otherwise), \emph{paraphrastic} if the onset fired but no clean period
exists, and otherwise short-fragment, single-token, or no-onset. Fractions in
\S\ref{sec:tax} are over all truncated traces in the full $252\times10$ run.

\paragraph{Examples (Qwen3-8B, task 117).} Two samples of the \emph{same} problem truncate in the two
modes. The verbatim sample repeats a block identically, period after period:
\begin{quote}\small\itshape
``$\ldots$At this point, I'm stuck. I think I need to look for a different approach. Maybe the problem
can be solved by considering the segments as walls and using a BFS to find the connected regions. But
how to represent the regions? Perhaps the problem can be modeled as a graph where each node is a
segment, and the edges represent adjacency. But I'm not sure.'' \textrm{[recurs verbatim on a fixed
period]}
\end{quote}
The paraphrastic sample instead recurs \emph{aperiodically}: a core clause repeats, but the framing
around it permutes, so no single period matches and the $n$-gram/period detectors do not lock on:
\begin{quote}\small\itshape
``$\ldots$\textrm{[pass 1]} \underline{Given the time constraints}, I'll proceed with writing a code
that checks if the cow can reach infinity $\ldots$ If not, compute the area. But I'm not sure how to
implement this. $\ldots$ \textrm{[pass 2]} \underline{Finally}, I'll write a code that checks if the
cow can reach infinity $\ldots$ If not, compute the area. But I'm not sure how to implement this.
$\ldots$ \textrm{[pass 3]} \underline{At this point, I'm unable to proceed further.} I think I need to
give up and write a code that $\ldots$''
\end{quote}
This illustrates why paraphrastic loops evade period-based and hidden-state-precursor detectors
(\S\ref{sec:tax}): the repetition is real but aperiodic. It is \emph{not} a claim of ``no repetition''
--- an embedding-similarity detector built for non-verbatim repetition (untested here) is the relevant
candidate.
