### **Karrtik Iyer**

Hyperparameter-Free Decoding for Code Generation

The current wave of agentic AI is rapidly converging on code generation as a primary application — agents that write, debug, and refactor code autonomously. A key architectural pattern in these systems is to generate multiple candidate solutions and select the best one, making the ability to produce diverse yet correct outputs a first-class requirement. Small language models (SLMs, 1B–7B parameters) are especially attractive for this role: their low latency, reduced cost, and suitability for local or on-device deployment make it practical to generate many candidates per task. However, current decoding strategies — temperature, nucleus (top-p), beam search — all require careful per-model hyperparameter tuning to balance correctness against diversity. In this project, we evaluate whether **p-less sampling**, a hyperparameter-free method based on collision entropy thresholding, can deliver a better accuracy–diversity trade-off out of the box. P-less dynamically derives its truncation threshold from the model's own output distribution at each decoding step, removing the need for per-model tuning entirely.

Over the past month, we expanded our evaluation to **14 models** across **192 configurations** on MBPP (500 tasks × 10 samples) and HumanEval (164 tasks × 10 samples), covering the Qwen, CodeLlama, Llama-2, and Codestral families in both base and instruct variants. The central finding is that p-less consistently delivers higher first-sample accuracy than temperature and top-p sampling, particularly on capable code models: on MBPP, Qwen2.5-Coder-3B achieves **59.4% pass@1** with p-less-norm versus **42.6%** for temperature (+16.8pp), and on HumanEval, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct reaches **85.4% pass@1** versus **77.9%** for temperature (+7.5pp). Critically, p-less captures 77–90% of its pass@10 potential in a single sample, compared to only 38–69% for temperature — a significant advantage for production settings where inference budgets limit generation to 1–3 attempts. We also introduced several new evaluation dimensions this month: greedy, beam search, and top-k baselines; CodeBLEU-based diversity metrics; a post-truncation temperature (T2) mechanism that flattens the survivor distribution after p-less pruning; and a two-stage temperature mechanism (T1/T2) for instruct models. A notable finding is that p-less produces near-zero structural diversity on instruct-tuned models (diversity < 0.02 at standard temperatures), indicating effectively deterministic decoding — the T1/T2 mechanism was designed to address this by opening the peaked instruct distribution before p-less pruning.

On base models, p-less at temperature 1.0 occupies a Pareto-optimal position: it buys diversity 3–5× more cheaply per point of pass@1 lost than temperature sampling (e.g. 0.089 CodeBLEU-diversity/pp for p-less vs 0.029/pp for temperature on Qwen2.5-Coder-3B), making it the preferred method when some output variety is needed without paying a steep accuracy cost. On instruct models, however, p-less produces near-deterministic outputs at standard temperatures. Our T1 (pre-truncation temperature) sweep shows that raising T1 to 1.5–2.0 restores meaningful diversity — on Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct, T1=1.5 yields 76.7% pass@1 with 0.13 structural diversity (versus 0.06 at T1=1.0), costing only 0.5pp. At matched diversity levels, p-less and temperature perform comparably on instruct models, so the Pareto advantage is specific to base models. Looking ahead, we are investigating an AST-phase-aware decoding approach that assigns different sampling temperatures to different structural phases of generated code (function signatures, body expressions, operators). An initial phase-oracle probe on one model and benchmark will determine whether the per-phase entropy variation is large enough to justify building a full phase-aware decoder.
