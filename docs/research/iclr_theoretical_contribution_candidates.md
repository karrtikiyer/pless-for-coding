# ICLR Theory-Track Contribution Candidates for Rényi-α p-less on Code

**Author note (skeptical-realist framing).** Tan, Wu, and Howard ([arXiv:2509.23234v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23234), ICLR 2026 oral) already own (i) the Rényi-α generalization of p-less, (ii) the asymptotic limits `k→0 ⇒ uniform`, `k→∞ ⇒ greedy`, (iii) monotonicity of the admission set in `k`, and (iv) qualitative "robustness to high temperature" claims. Any theoretical contribution we put forward for an ICLR *full-paper* theory submission must clear a high bar: it must add something beyond algebraic restatement of what Rényi entropy already gives for free. This document brainstorms 10 candidate directions, applies a strict pruning filter, and ends with a single concrete recommendation. Cited literature is real; where I cannot sketch a proof I say so.

## Pruning filter (applied throughout)

A candidate survives only if it satisfies ALL of:

- **(N)** *Non-trivial:* not a one-line corollary of Rényi entropy monotonicity, of `Σ pᵢ^α` decreasing in α, of Jensen's inequality, or of Tan et al.'s already-stated asymptotics.
- **(F)** *Falsifiable:* it predicts a *quantitative* observable on our data or on data we can produce in ≤ 2 weeks.
- **(E)** *Explanatory:* it accounts for an empirical phenomenon we observed (monotone α-lift, T-cliff, bimodal entropy, NAUADC lift, no α-cliff through α=5) rather than merely describes it.
- **(P)** *Provable, or near-provable:* I can sketch the argument in 3–8 sentences. "I think this is true" without a sketch is rejected.
- **(O)** *Original:* not already in Tan et al. App. B.5/C.7, not in [Meister et al. 2022 (locally typical sampling, arXiv:2202.00666)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00666), [Nguyen et al. 2024 (min-p, arXiv:2407.01082)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01082), [Hewitt et al. 2022 (η-sampling / desmoothing, arXiv:2210.15191)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15191), or [Finkelstein & Vechev 2024 (REAL sampling, TACL)](https://direct.mit.edu/tacl/article/doi/10.1162/tacl_a_00757/131836).

## The ten brainstorm candidates

### C1. Bimodal next-token entropy as a theorem from PCFG + BPE

**Statement.** Let `L` be a programming language generated by a probabilistic context-free grammar `G` whose nonterminal expansion entropies fall into two natural classes: *structural positions* (token must be a keyword/punctuation given the leftmost-derivation state) and *content positions* (identifiers, literals, operands). Suppose tokenization is BPE-trained on a corpus drawn i.i.d. from `G`. Then for any model that minimizes cross-entropy against this corpus, the conditional next-token entropy `H₂(p · | context)` is asymptotically (as the corpus → ∞ and as BPE merges → ∞) a mixture of two atoms with vanishing variance: one near `0` (structural) and one near `log(|content vocab|)` (content). I.e., bimodality is a theorem about *the data*, inherited by *any* sufficiently expressive model.

**Why not obvious.** PCFGs have Shannon entropies of expansion rules ([Chi 1999](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/973215.973219)), but the bimodality claim is about the *position-level* induced distribution after BPE, not the rule-level entropy. BPE re-aggregates content positions but should not affect structural positions; the interaction is non-trivial.

**Predicts/explains.** Explains our Hartigan dip-test result (`p≈0`, dip≈0.005–0.013 on Qwen + CodeLlama). Predicts: (a) dip statistic should *increase* with model accuracy on code (better models match the PCFG better); (b) dip statistic should be *higher* on highly-typed languages (Rust, Java) than on dynamically-typed (Python); (c) dip statistic should *vanish* on natural language. (c) is a sharp falsifier — we already have entropy logging infrastructure, can run a small dip-test on a 50-prompt CNN/DailyMail subset.

**Proof sketch.** Construct PCFG `G`. Let `q_s, q_c` be the marginal probabilities that a position is structural vs content. At structural positions, expansion rule entropy is near zero by definition; the token distribution concentrates on one BPE token. At content positions, identifiers are drawn from a Zipfian-Mandelbrot lexicon (empirical regularity, [Pierret & Latapy 2007](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221200132)), whose entropy is `Θ(log V)` where `V` is the effective lexicon. BPE merges rare tokens but cannot reduce the entropy of a Zipfian source below its rate (Shannon source coding theorem). A model minimizing cross-entropy must converge in `L²` to this true distribution on every position, so its `H₂(p · | context)` inherits the two-atom structure.

**Literature priors.** PCFG entropy is well-studied (Chi 1999); Zipfian source code is from Pierret & Latapy 2007. Bimodal *empirical* entropy in code is in the related "challenging vs confident token" literature ([Zhu et al. 2023, "Hot or Cold", arXiv:2309.02772](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02772); [DecoRTL 2025, arXiv:2507.02226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02226)). But none of these derive bimodality from a generative model. Tan et al. do *not* address this.

**Feasibility.** Proof sketch is feasible. Need: clean PCFG-with-BPE formalism (~1 week), Theorem 1 statement and proof (~1 week). Empirical falsifier: 1 day (dip test on natural language to confirm bimodality is code-specific).

**Honest novelty.** Strong if we get the formal statement right. PCFG-based theorems about LM behaviour have a clean precedent ([Liu et al. 2024 "PCFG-based uncertainty"](https://huggingface.co/papers?q=Context-Free+Grammar)) but none target the bimodal-entropy claim. **A theory reviewer would find this interesting because it bridges programming-language theory and LM decoding.**

**Verdict: KEEP.**

### C2. Critical-T phase transition from rate-distortion

**Statement.** For a vocabulary `V` with effective support `s(p)` (number of tokens with `p ≥ 1/|V|`), define the *critical temperature* `T_c(p) := H₁(p) / log s(p)`. Claim: for any auto-regressive LM, expected pass@1 satisfies `pass@1(T) ≥ pass@1(0) − c · max(0, T − T_c)²` for some model-independent `c > 0`. I.e., the T-curve is locally quadratic in the deviation from `T_c`, and `T > T_c` is where the cliff begins.

**Why not obvious.** Temperature scaling is studied empirically (Holtzman et al. 2019; Nguyen et al. 2024) but I am unaware of any closed-form prediction of *where* the cliff occurs. [Nakaishi et al. 2024 (arXiv:2406.05335)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05335) prove a *qualitative* phase transition for LLMs as `T` rises but their critical-T is empirically estimated, not predicted from `(p, V)`.

**Predicts/explains.** Predicts our observed cliff between T=2.5 and T=3.0 from per-position entropy statistics alone. Falsifier: compute `T_c(p)` averaged over positions for Qwen, CodeLlama, m-a-p; check whether `T_c ∈ [2.5, 3.0]` for all three models. We have the entropy logs (commit 5492324).

**Proof sketch.** Heuristic, not yet rigorous. At temperature `T`, the softmax-tempered distribution `p_T(i) ∝ p(i)^{1/T}` has Rényi-1 entropy `H₁(p_T) = T · H₁(p) − T · D_{KL}(p_T ‖ p)`. As `T → ∞`, `p_T` approaches uniform on the support, so the probability of selecting the correct token approaches `1/s`. The "cliff" should occur when `p_T(argmax p)` falls below the level needed to reliably select the modal-token, which happens around the point where `H₁(p_T)` exceeds `log s − O(1)`. This gives a critical temperature `T_c ≈ log s / H₁(p)`. The cliff is then driven by per-position multiplication of these survival probabilities across the generation length — a binomial-tail argument explains why a `−14 to −44 pp` *aggregate* cliff can come from a smooth per-token shift.

**Literature priors.** Nakaishi et al. 2024 prove phase transitions exist; they do not give `T_c` in closed form. [Maini et al. 2025 (Pass@k Diagnostic, arXiv:2511.16231)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16231) prove the gradient-collinearity result for pass@k vs pass@1 — relevant but tangential. **No prior paper predicts T_c from per-position statistics, AFAICT.**

**Feasibility.** Empirical falsifier: 1–2 days using our entropy logs. Proof of the locally-quadratic bound: probably 2–3 weeks, with risk of getting only a heuristic. The hard part is making the per-position bound aggregate cleanly over an autoregressive trajectory — autoregressive error propagation is a known wart in this literature.

**Honest novelty.** Strong predictor (`T_c` from `(p, V)`) is genuinely new. The bound itself is harder; might fall back to a weaker statement like `T_c ≤ log s / H₁(p)` (upper bound only).

**Verdict: KEEP, with risk that the bound becomes weaker than the heuristic suggests.**

### C3. Why α-sweep avoids the cliff: a survival theorem for the mode

**Statement.** For p-less-α with `α ≥ 2`, the modal token `i* = argmax p` is admitted at *every* position with probability 1, and the admission set's effective size `s_α := |{i : p_i ≥ Σ p_j^α}|` satisfies `s_α ≤ |V|^{1 − 1/α}`. Consequently, even at α = 5 the admission set is bounded *uniformly across positions* by `|V|^{4/5}`, and the modal token retains probability mass at least `1 − (|V|^{1−1/α} − 1) · Σ p_j^α / p_{i*}` after renormalization. This bound is independent of the entropy of the position.

**Why not obvious.** That the mode survives at `α=2` is one-line (`Σ p² ≤ max p`); the *uniform-in-position* bound `s_α ≤ |V|^{1−1/α}` is not. It comes from Hölder's inequality applied to `Σ_{i ∈ admitted} 1 ≤ (Σ p_i^α)^{−1/α} · (Σ p_i^{α/(α−1)})^{(α−1)/α} · (Σ 1)^?` — there's real combinatorial content here, not just an algebraic rewrite. **Need to double-check this bound; my sketch may be off by exponents.**

**Predicts/explains.** Explains why α=5 does not exhibit a cliff: the admission set stays bounded in a way that doesn't blow up with entropy. Predicts: there exists a finite α* (function of `|V|`) beyond which p-less-α *does* eventually behave like uniform sampling and a cliff appears. Falsifier: run α = {10, 20, 50, 100} on a small subset and check whether the cliff appears around the predicted α*.

**Proof sketch.** Mode survival: `p_{i*} ≥ p_{i*}^α / max p^{α-1} ≥ Σ p_j^α / max p^{α-1}`. For `α ≥ 2` and the mode `i*`, `p_{i*} ≥ p_{i*}^α` directly, since `p_{i*} ≤ 1`. Admission-set size: among admitted tokens, `p_i ≥ Σ p_j^α`. Then `s_α · Σ p_j^α ≤ Σ_{admitted} p_i ≤ 1`, so `s_α ≤ 1/Σ p_j^α`. By the power-mean inequality, `Σ p_j^α ≥ |V|^{1−α}` (uniform lower bound), giving `s_α ≤ |V|^{α−1}` — *worse* than I claimed above. The improved bound requires more work and is the non-trivial step.

**Literature priors.** The bound `s_α ≤ 1/Σ p_j^α = exp((α−1) H_α)` is essentially the *effective support size*, well-known in concentration inequalities ([Renner & Wolf, "smooth Rényi entropy"](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0bf0cbc4d7e49f2405f0b4bdd44834046de4a7f0)). What's new is connecting this to *temperature-cliff avoidance* via the autoregressive aggregation.

**Feasibility.** The mode-survival statement is easy. The cliff-avoidance bound and the prediction of `α*` is harder; 2 weeks of work to nail down, with risk the bound is too loose to be empirically interesting.

**Honest novelty.** The combinatorial bound is borderline known. The novel framing is *cliff avoidance ↔ effective support size of admitted set*. A theory reviewer might say "this is just smooth Rényi entropy applied to truncation." **Moderate. Probably workshop-grade rather than full-track ICLR.**

**Verdict: KEEP as supporting result, not as headline.**

### C4. Axiomatic uniqueness of the threshold family

**Statement.** Among all functionals `T: Δ^{|V|−1} → ℝ` that (i) are continuous, (ii) are permutation-invariant, (iii) are scale-equivariant in a precise sense, (iv) recover greedy as `H₂(p) → 0` and uniform as `p → uniform`, (v) satisfy a tensor-product compatibility for product distributions, the family `T_α(p) = Σ p_i^α` (or equivalently `1/exp(H_α(p))`) is the unique one-parameter family. This characterizes the Rényi-α p-less family as the *natural* hyperparameter-free truncation framework.

**Why not obvious.** Mirrors classical entropy axiomatizations ([Rényi 1961; Daróczy 1963; arXiv:1311.0324](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.0324)) but for *threshold functionals*, not entropies. The right set of axioms is itself a research contribution.

**Predicts/explains.** Doesn't predict anything new empirically. Pure structural result.

**Proof sketch.** Honest answer: I cannot sketch this in 3–8 sentences. The Rényi/Daróczy axiomatic program took years to settle. Adapting it to *truncation thresholds* rather than entropy measures requires reformulating axioms (iii)–(v) in a way that admits a uniqueness proof. The recent [arXiv:2410.15976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15976) (quantum-phase-space Rényi axiomatization, 2024) gives a partial template but doesn't transfer directly. **I cannot sketch a proof. Reject candidate unless we are willing to spend 1–2 months developing the axiomatic framework.**

**Literature priors.** Heavy existing literature on entropy axiomatization. None target truncation thresholds for sampling.

**Feasibility.** Low. Risk of producing a result that the community calls "elegant but obvious." Worse, ICLR theory reviewers often reject pure axiomatizations as "math repackaging."

**Honest novelty.** The framing is novel but the result might collapse to a restatement of Rényi's theorem. **Reject.**

**Verdict: REJECT.**

### C5. Provable Pareto-dominance over the temperature curve

**Statement.** Let `f: Δ → ℝ²` be the (pass@1, pass@10) frontier achievable by temperature sampling at temperatures `T ∈ [0, ∞)`. Let `g_α` be the frontier of p-less-α at fixed α and sweeping over `T_external`. Claim: under a *bimodality condition* on the per-position next-token distribution (formalized as: there exists ε such that ≥ 50% of positions have `H₂(p) < ε` and ≥ 10% have `H₂(p) > log |V| − ε`), there exists `α₀ < ∞` such that for all `α ≥ α₀`, `g_α` Pareto-dominates `f` strictly on a positive-measure subset of the frontier.

**Why not obvious.** This is a *conditional* theorem (conditional on the bimodality structure we observe). The challenge is: *why* should bimodality plus high-α produce dominance? Intuition: at low-entropy positions α-truncation keeps only the mode (helping pass@1); at high-entropy positions α-truncation admits many tokens (helping pass@10). Temperature scaling cannot do both because it acts uniformly across positions.

**Predicts/explains.** Directly explains our observed Pareto dominance on 6/6 cells. Predicts: on a synthetic *uni*modal distribution (constant `H₂` per position) the dominance should *vanish*. Falsifier: synthesize an LM with uniformly-mid-entropy positions (a fine-tuned model on a "creative writing" task perhaps) and check whether α-sweep still Pareto-dominates. We could approximate this with the m-a-p numbers (lowest dip stat) — though m-a-p still shows monotonicity in our data.

**Proof sketch.** Decompose generation as alternating "structural" (low-entropy) and "content" (high-entropy) positions. At structural positions, temperature acts as a multiplicative penalty on the gap `p_{i*} − p_second`, scaling pass@1 down as `T` rises; α-pless leaves the mode untouched. At content positions, temperature widens the tail uniformly, including many wrong tokens; α-pless admits only tokens above a threshold that scales with `H_α(p)`. Construct independent-position model with explicit entropy distribution; compute pass@1 and pass@10 in closed form as a function of `T` and `α`; show the α-frontier strictly dominates for some range. **This is technical but tractable — maybe 3 weeks.**

**Literature priors.** [Liu et al. 2025 (sampling-diversity scaling, arXiv:2502.11027)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11027) discuss diversity-quality tradeoffs; do not give Pareto-dominance theorems. Tan et al. 2025 claim Pareto dominance empirically on math/QA without a theorem.

**Feasibility.** Moderate. Independent-position assumption is the obvious simplification; need to verify it doesn't trivialize. If the independent-position model collapses to "α just rescales temperature," the theorem dies — need to check with synthetic data first.

**Honest novelty.** Strong if it works. *A theorem connecting per-position entropy bimodality to a sampler's Pareto dominance over temperature* is a clean, ICLR-grade result. **A theory reviewer would care.**

**Verdict: KEEP as headline candidate.**

### C6. Rényi-divergence unification of typical / top-p / min-p / p-less

**Statement.** Locally typical sampling [Meister et al. 2022, arXiv:2202.00666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00666), top-p (nucleus), min-p [Nguyen et al. 2024, arXiv:2407.01082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01082), and p-less-α are all instances of a *single* truncation rule parameterized by Rényi order `α`: "admit token `i` iff the Rényi-α divergence of (truncated) from (original) below threshold τ is minimal over admission sets." Specifically: top-p ↔ α=1 (Shannon), p-less ↔ α=2 (collision), min-p ↔ α=∞, typical ↔ a different α-projection. We characterize each method as a projection in α-divergence geometry.

**Why not obvious.** Classifying samplers under one framework is appealing but easy to mis-state. The Rényi-divergence framework is well-developed ([arXiv:1602.02311 variational inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.02311); [Sason & Verdú 2018 information-theoretic bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03417)). What's not done: characterizing *truncation samplers* as Rényi projections.

**Predicts/explains.** Predicts an interpolating family `α ∈ [1, ∞]` that smoothly connects top-p, p-less, and min-p. Falsifier: run this interpolating family on a benchmark and check whether α=2 (p-less) is on the Pareto frontier (we already know it is), and whether the interpolation curves smoothly. Could also predict: top-p with a *self-derived* `p_target = Σ p^α` is equivalent to p-less-α.

**Proof sketch.** Top-p admits `{i : p_i ∈ top set summing to p}`, equivalently solves "min |S| s.t. Σ_S p ≥ p" — a linear cardinality constraint. Min-p admits `{i : p_i ≥ p_floor · max p}` — a max-prob ratio. These don't share an obvious Rényi-α form, so this might be wishful framing. **I am not confident I can sketch a clean proof. The relationship may exist only between *some* pairs, not all four.**

**Literature priors.** [Chang & Tang 2026 (Markov categorical framework, arXiv:2507.19247)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19247) gives a category-theoretic unification of LLMs but does not address samplers. No paper unifies typical/top-p/min-p/p-less under Rényi divergences AFAICT — but the absence might be because it doesn't cleanly work.

**Feasibility.** Risky. If the unification "works" only for 2/4 methods we have a weaker, narrower result. **Likely a 2-month detour with uncertain payoff.**

**Honest novelty.** Would be high if successful, but I suspect the framework will be partial. ICLR theory reviewers will spot a forced unification.

**Verdict: REJECT for ICLR — keep as a workshop/blog post idea.**

### C7. Pass@k–vs-α curve from independent-position correctness

**Statement.** Under an *independent-positions correctness assumption* (a sample is correct iff every position's sampled token is in the correct set; correctness events independent across positions), pass@k under p-less-α has the closed form `pass@k(α) = 1 − E_p[(1 − q_α(p))^k]^L` where `q_α(p) = (Σ_{i ∈ correct ∩ admitted} p_i) / (Σ_{i ∈ admitted} p_i)`, `L` is the generation length, and the expectation is over the per-position entropy distribution. We derive sufficient conditions on the per-position distribution for `∂_α pass@k > 0` and `∂_α pass@1 ≤ 0`, and show the *crossing point* `α*` where pass@10 saturates depends explicitly on the bimodality of the per-position entropy distribution.

**Why not obvious.** Pass@k is normally measured empirically. A closed-form expression that *predicts* the (pass@1, pass@10)-curve from per-position entropy statistics is novel. The independence assumption is strong but is the standard simplification (it's also the assumption underlying the original [Chen et al. 2021 Codex pass@k estimator, arXiv:2107.03374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374)).

**Predicts/explains.** Mechanistically explains the monotone pass@10 lift in our data and the bounded pass@1 cost. Predicts: (a) the pass@10 gain from α should *scale linearly* with the fraction of high-entropy ("content") positions; (b) the pass@1 cost should scale with the fraction of low-entropy positions where the *second-best* token is incorrect. Falsifiers: (a) measure correlation of pass@10 gain (α=5 vs α=2) with our dip-statistic across models; (b) measure correlation of pass@1 cost with the conditional `P(top2 wrong | low entropy)`.

**Proof sketch.** Pass@k = `1 − P(all k samples wrong)`. Per-position, `P(wrong | admitted set, α) = 1 − q_α`. By independence assumption and length `L`, `P(all positions correct in one sample) = E_p[q_α(p)^L]`. Then `P(all k samples wrong)` = `E_p[(1 − q_α(p))^k]^L` (factoring sample-independence). Differentiating in α: as α grows, the admission set shrinks, so `q_α` increases at low-entropy positions (admission shrinks toward mode = correct) and decreases at high-entropy positions (admission still wide). The two effects oppose each other; their balance gives the α* prediction.

**Literature priors.** Chen et al. 2021 pass@k estimator is for unbiased estimation, not for prediction from `(p, α)`. [Yuan et al. 2025 (Pass@k Training, arXiv:2508.10751)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10751) and the diagnostic paper [arXiv:2511.16231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16231) discuss pass@k from an RL-objective perspective. *None give a closed-form prediction from per-position entropy statistics.*

**Feasibility.** High. Independence assumption + our existing entropy logs + correctness labels. Can verify the predicted scaling laws in 1 week. Proof of monotonicity is direct from the formula.

**Honest novelty.** This is a genuine theoretical contribution: *the first analytical model of pass@k as a function of sampler parameter, predicting empirical observations from measurable per-position statistics.* **An ICLR theory reviewer would find this both elegant and testable.**

**Verdict: KEEP as headline candidate.**

### C8. Maximum-entropy characterization of p-less-α

**Statement.** The renormalized p-less-α distribution `p̃_α` is the maximum-Shannon-entropy distribution subject to (i) support is a subset of `{i : p_i ≥ Σ p_j^α}`, (ii) the constraint `Σ_i p̃_α(i) · 1[p_i < T_α] = 0` (no admitted-set leakage), and (iii) `KL(p̃_α ‖ p) ≤ τ_α` for the Rényi-α-determined slack `τ_α`. This characterizes p-less-α as the principled max-ent sampler under an α-determined fidelity constraint.

**Why not obvious.** Max-ent characterizations are routine for entropy measures (Jaynes) but not for truncation rules. The right constraint set is the contribution.

**Predicts/explains.** Doesn't predict anything new empirically. Structural characterization only.

**Proof sketch.** Direct Lagrangian. Maximize `H₁(p̃)` over admissible distributions with KL constraint to `p`. Optimal `p̃` is renormalized `p` on the support — which is exactly p-less. Then show `τ_α = KL(p̃_α ‖ p)` matches the Rényi-α slack. **One-page proof, mostly mechanical.**

**Literature priors.** Standard max-ent / Jaynes program. Nothing new at the technical level.

**Feasibility.** Trivial.

**Honest novelty.** Low. A theory reviewer would say "this is the standard max-ent argument for any truncation rule." **Reject.**

**Verdict: REJECT.**

### C9. Lower bound on low-entropy token fraction in code from BPE + grammar

**Statement.** For any Python program in a representative corpus, the fraction of generation positions with `H₂(p · | context) < ε log |V|` is at least `1 − f(ε, |V|, BPE-vocab-size, grammar-rule-entropy)`. We give an explicit `f` depending only on grammar statistics. In particular, for typical Python+BPE-30k, at least 40% of positions have collision entropy below 0.1 bits.

**Why not obvious.** Empirically observed (Zhu et al. 2023) but never *proved* from grammar properties. This is a quantitative claim about the code distribution itself, not about any specific model.

**Predicts/explains.** Predicts the fraction of "confident tokens" reported in AdapT (Zhu et al. 2023). Falsifier: estimate `f(ε, |V|, BPE, grammar)` from a Python AST analysis, compare to empirical fraction. We have empirical entropies logged.

**Proof sketch.** Decompose Python tokens by AST node type. Structural tokens (`def`, `:`, `(`, indent) have effectively deterministic continuations given parser state — entropy = 0 by grammar. BPE doesn't split these. So a lower bound on the fraction of low-entropy positions reduces to a fraction-of-structural-tokens claim about Python. Empirically Python source is ~30–50% structural punctuation/keyword by token count (we can verify with `tokenize` module). Therefore `f` is bounded below by the structural-token fraction.

**Literature priors.** Pierret & Latapy 2007 (Zipf for code); Allamanis et al. 2018 (survey of ML on code); Zhu et al. 2023 (confident vs challenging tokens empirically). **No paper proves a lower bound on confident-token fraction from grammar.**

**Feasibility.** Easy — 1 week to formalize and verify on Python AST data.

**Honest novelty.** Moderate. A theory reviewer might say "this is a fact about Python, not about decoding." It's a *supporting* result for C1 and C5, but not a headline.

**Verdict: KEEP as supporting lemma for C1/C5/C7, not as a standalone contribution.**

### C10. Optimal α(T) schedule from per-position entropy

**Statement.** Given a per-position entropy estimator `Ĥ(context)`, the *optimal* α-schedule `α*(T, Ĥ)` that maximizes pass@k at fixed pass@1 satisfies `α*(T, Ĥ) = c · T · Ĥ` for some model-dependent `c`, derivable from the Pass@k closed form in C7. We derive `α*` analytically and propose a *position-adaptive* p-less-α^(t) sampler that strictly Pareto-dominates fixed-α p-less.

**Why not obvious.** Adaptive temperature schedules exist (AdapT, Zhu et al. 2023) but require token classification. An *entropy-derived* α schedule is a different beast: it requires the C7 closed form to know what's optimal.

**Predicts/explains.** Predicts that an adaptive sampler should beat any fixed α we've tried so far. Falsifier: implement adaptive `α*(t)`, run on MBPP, check whether it Pareto-dominates α=5.

**Proof sketch.** From C7 closed form, set `∂_α pass@k(α | Ĥ) / ∂_α pass@1(α | Ĥ) = λ` (frontier slope), solve for α. Get `α*(Ĥ) = ...`. Without C7 first, this candidate has no foundation.

**Literature priors.** AdapT (Zhu et al. 2023); DecoRTL 2025 (position-aware decoding for RTL). Neither uses Rényi α; both classify tokens externally.

**Feasibility.** *Depends on C7.* If C7 succeeds, C10 is a 2-week extension that converts theory into an algorithm. If C7 fails, C10 has no anchor.

**Honest novelty.** High if anchored on C7. **A theorem + an algorithm + an empirical demonstration is exactly what an ICLR full paper looks like.**

**Verdict: KEEP, conditional on C7 succeeding.**

## Concrete recommendation

For a *bona-fide ICLR full-track submission* (theory + empirical), the natural backbone is **C7 → C10**, with **C1 and C9** as supporting lemmas and **C5** as a strengthening Pareto theorem.

**The single-paragraph thesis we should aim for:**

> *Code-generation next-token distributions are statistically bimodal in collision entropy (proved from a PCFG+BPE model in Theorem 1; empirically confirmed via Hartigan's dip test on 280K positions). Under independent-positions correctness (the standard pass@k assumption), this bimodality predicts a monotone pass@10 lift in α and a bounded pass@1 cost (Theorem 2, closed-form pass@k(α) curve). The closed-form pass@k(α) curve further specifies an optimal context-adaptive α(t) schedule (Theorem 3) that strictly Pareto-dominates fixed-α p-less. We empirically validate on 3 code-specialized models × 2 benchmarks × 4 α settings.*

This gives the reviewer (i) a new theorem about *code* (C1), (ii) a new theorem about *samplers on code* (C7), (iii) a new *algorithm* derived from (ii) (C10), and (iv) empirical validation we already have for the C7 prediction and would need to add for C10.

**Realistic timeline.**

- C7 closed-form derivation + empirical-correlation falsifiers: **2 weeks** (high confidence).
- C1 PCFG+BPE bimodality theorem: **3 weeks** (medium confidence; might end up as a "model-of-code theorem" lemma rather than a hero result).
- C10 adaptive-α algorithm + small benchmark validation: **2 weeks** *after* C7.
- C5 conditional Pareto-dominance theorem: **3 weeks** (medium confidence; could collapse to a corollary of C7).
- Writing: **2 weeks**.

**Total: ~10–12 weeks for a credible ICLR full-paper submission.** This is ICLR-cycle-feasible only if we start by **June 2026** for the **September 2026 ICLR 2027 deadline**.

**If we have less than that:** The honest answer is that without C7 and C1 nailed, we have a *workshop paper* — a strong empirical study with mild theoretical framing (essentially what we have now, plus a careful citation of Tan et al.). The empirical findings (monotone α-lift on code, T-cliff at 2.5→3.0, NAUADC monotonicity, dip-test bimodality) are publishable on their own at an ICLR workshop or a top-tier code-LLM venue (NAACL/EMNLP findings, ML4SE workshop). **They are not yet ICLR-theory-track.**

**Single concrete next step.** Derive the closed-form pass@k(α) under independent-positions correctness (C7). It is the cheapest theorem to attempt, the most directly testable on our existing data (entropy logs + correctness labels per position), and the precondition for the algorithmic contribution C10. If C7 yields a clean prediction that matches our empirical α-lift within a tight error bar, we have an ICLR-theory submission. If it doesn't, we have a paper that's interesting but not theory-track, and we should aim for ICLR's empirical track or a workshop.
